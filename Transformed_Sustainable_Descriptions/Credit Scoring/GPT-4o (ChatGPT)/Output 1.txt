The sales clerks in a bank can use their energy-efficient software frontend, optimized to reduce unnecessary processing, to receive the credit-scoring for a certain customer. This starts a digitalized process in the banking system, which communicates with the agency using green, cloud-based services that operate on servers powered by renewable energy sources.

This process sends a scoring request to the agency via a secure, energy-efficient API connection immediately after the request is initiated. The agency then performs a first quick scoring (level 1), which is designed to minimize computational energy consumption. This fast process often leads to an immediate result, which is returned digitally to the banking system within seconds, minimizing the environmental footprint by avoiding unnecessary data storage or transport. The banking process presents the result to the clerk in real-time on the low-energy consumption frontend.

In cases where the scoring cannot be determined immediately and requires more intensive processing, the agency informs the banking process about the delay. The delay notification is sent using optimized communication protocols that minimize bandwidth use, ensuring lower energy consumption across the network. The agency then begins the level 2 scoring, which is processed using servers powered by renewable energy, and the algorithm is designed to balance processing speed with energy efficiency. This second scoring may take up to a couple of minutes. 

The scoring result, once determined, is sent back digitally to the banking process using the same optimized, eco-friendly data channels. During the delay period, the banking process displays a message to the clerk at the frontend, informing them to check the system later for updates. This digital notification reduces the need for unnecessary system queries, further lowering the energy footprint. 

As soon as the result arrives, it is immediately available for the clerk on the energy-efficient software frontend. Throughout the process, all system components—from frontend to backend—utilize green software principles, prioritizing low energy consumption, minimal data transmission, and efficient use of resources. No physical materials are used, as all communications are electronic, and the entire process is managed by systems that utilize recycled hardware components and renewable energy sources, ensuring a low overall carbon footprint.
