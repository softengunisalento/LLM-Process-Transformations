In the bank's credit-scoring process, the sales clerks can digitally access the software frontend to initiate the credit-scoring for a customer. This interaction triggers a sustainable process within the banking system, which is optimized for low energy consumption and reduced environmental impact. Upon receiving the request, the process seamlessly communicates with the agency via a digital interface, avoiding unnecessary physical actions.

Instead of relying on servers with high carbon footprints, the bank and the agency use energy-efficient data centers powered by renewable energy sources, such as solar or wind energy. The scoring request is transmitted instantly and digitally to the agency using secure, low-power communication protocols, minimizing energy usage during data transfer.

Once the request reaches the agency, a quick digital scoring process (level 1) is performed using green software that prioritizes efficient algorithms designed to minimize CPU cycles, thus reducing energy consumption. If the scoring result is immediately available, it is sent back digitally to the banking system within seconds. The result is promptly displayed to the sales clerk through the energy-efficient frontend, built on low-power, resource-optimized software platforms.

In cases where the initial scoring cannot be completed instantly, the agency sends an automated message, powered by green software, to the banking system, informing them of the delay. During this period, the level 2 scoring is processed using energy-efficient hardware and algorithms that aim to reduce computational energy use. The agency utilizes energy-saving practices, such as load balancing across low-emission servers and automatic resource scaling, ensuring that no excess energy is consumed when demand is low.

Once the final scoring result is generated, it is digitally sent back to the banking process through encrypted, low-energy communication methods. The banking process, which operates on a platform optimized for minimal environmental impact, displays a notification to the clerk, indicating the delay and prompting them to check back later. When the result is received, it appears immediately on the digital interface without the need for manual intervention or additional resources.

Throughout the entire process, actions that were previously performed manually or required physical infrastructure have been fully digitalized to reduce waste and emissions. The bank and agency ensure that the software and hardware involved in the process are part of a circular economy, where electronic components are recycled, and hardware is reused or repurposed to prevent e-waste.

This optimized process significantly reduces the overall carbon footprint by eliminating unnecessary physical transportation, minimizing energy consumption through green computing practices, and leveraging renewable energy sources to power all digital operations.
