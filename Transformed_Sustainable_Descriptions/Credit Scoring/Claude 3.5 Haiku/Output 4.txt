Here's a more sustainable version of the BPMN process description, incorporating environmentally friendly practices, digitalization, and green software principles:

The sales clerks in a bank use energy-efficient, cloud-based software to request credit-scoring for a customer. This initiates a process in the bank's eco-friendly data center, which communicates with the agency via a secure, low-energy network connection. The process sends a digital scoring request to the agency immediately upon initiation. The agency then conducts an initial rapid scoring (level 1) using machine learning algorithms optimized for energy efficiency. This often results in an immediate outcome, which is transmitted back to the banking system within seconds via the same low-energy network. The banking process presents the result to the clerk through their energy-efficient display, which uses auto-brightness adjustment to conserve power. If the scoring cannot be determined instantly and requires more time, the agency notifies the banking process of the delay through an automated message. The agency then initiates the level 2 scoring, utilizing distributed computing resources during off-peak hours to minimize energy consumption. This process can take up to a couple of minutes but is significantly more energy-efficient than traditional methods. Once the scoring result is determined, the information is securely transmitted back to the banking process using encrypted, compressed data to reduce bandwidth usage. The banking process sends a push notification to the clerk's device, eliminating the need for continuous polling and reducing energy consumption. The clerk can then access the result through their energy-efficient frontend, which employs dark mode and optimized rendering to minimize power usage. Throughout this process, all data centers and computing resources involved utilize renewable energy sources such as solar or wind power. The software systems are designed with green coding practices, including efficient algorithms, data compression, and smart caching to reduce processing power and storage requirements. Regular energy audits are conducted on the entire system to identify and eliminate any wasteful processes or redundant data storage. Additionally, the bank and agency have implemented paperless operations, with all reports and documentation stored and shared digitally, further reducing the carbon footprint associated with physical document production and transportation. The hardware used in this process is sourced from manufacturers committed to sustainable practices and is designed for longevity and easy recycling at the end of its lifecycle. By implementing these sustainable practices and focusing on green software development, the bank and agency significantly reduce their environmental impact while maintaining efficient and secure credit-scoring processes.
