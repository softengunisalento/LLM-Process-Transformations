Here's a more sustainable version of the BPMN process description, incorporating energy efficiency, digitalization, and green software practices:

The sales clerks in a bank use energy-efficient, cloud-based software to request credit scoring for a customer. This initiates a process in the bank's green data center, which communicates with the agency via a secure, low-energy network. The process sends a digital scoring request to the agency immediately after initiation. The agency then performs an initial quick scoring (level 1) using machine learning algorithms optimized for energy efficiency. This often leads to an immediate result, which is returned directly to the banking system within seconds via encrypted data transmission. The banking process presents the result to the clerk on their energy-efficient, EPEAT-certified device with an eco-friendly display.

In cases where the scoring cannot be determined immediately and requires more time, the agency informs the banking process of the delay through an automated notification system. The agency then initiates the level 2 scoring using distributed computing resources powered by renewable energy sources. This process can take up to a couple of minutes but significantly reduces the carbon footprint compared to traditional methods. Once the scoring result is determined, the information is sent back to the banking process through a green network infrastructure that optimizes data transfer routes to minimize energy consumption.

The banking process automatically notifies the clerk about the delay through a pop-up message on their energy-efficient device, prompting them to check again later. The system uses smart caching and data compression techniques to reduce unnecessary data transfer and storage. As soon as the result arrives, it becomes instantly visible on the clerk's frontend, which is designed with green UX principles to minimize screen time and reduce device battery consumption.

Throughout this process, all data centers and computing resources involved utilize renewable energy sources such as solar or wind power. The software systems are designed with green coding practices, including efficient algorithms, optimized database queries, and automatic scaling to match computing resources with demand. This approach significantly reduces energy consumption during idle periods. Additionally, the bank and agency regularly audit their digital processes to identify and eliminate any redundant or energy-intensive operations, ensuring continuous improvement in the sustainability of their credit scoring system.
