Here's a more sustainable version of the BPMN process description, incorporating green practices and digitalization:

The sales clerks in a bank can use their energy-efficient software frontend, optimized for low power consumption, to receive the credit-scoring for a certain customer. This initiates a process in the banking system's eco-friendly cloud infrastructure, which communicates with the agency digitally in the background, eliminating the need for paper-based communications or physical transportation.

This process sends a secure, encrypted scoring request to the agency immediately after initiation, utilizing green networking protocols that minimize data transfer and energy usage. The agency then conducts an initial quick scoring (level 1) using machine learning algorithms optimized for energy efficiency. This often leads to an immediate result, which is then returned directly to the banking system within seconds via a low-energy data transfer. The banking process presents the result to the clerk on their energy-efficient frontend, which uses adaptive brightness and power-saving modes to reduce electricity consumption.

In cases where the scoring cannot be determined immediately and requires more time, the agency informs the banking process of the delay through an automated, low-bandwidth notification. The agency then initiates the level 2 scoring, which employs more complex, yet energy-optimized algorithms that can take up to a couple of minutes. These algorithms are designed to run on servers powered by renewable energy sources, such as solar or wind power. After the scoring result is determined, the information is sent back to the banking system using green data transmission protocols that prioritize energy efficiency over speed when appropriate.

The banking process displays a message to the clerk on their eco-friendly screen when it receives information about the delay, prompting them to check again later. This notification system is designed to minimize unnecessary computational processes and reduce energy consumption during waiting periods. As soon as the result arrives, it can be viewed on the frontend, which uses e-ink or OLED technology to further reduce power usage during display.

Throughout this process, all data centers and computational resources utilize advanced cooling systems that recycle waste heat for office climate control or other purposes. The software systems involved are built using green coding practices, such as efficient algorithms, dark mode interfaces, and compressed data storage, to minimize computational resources and energy consumption. Regular carbon footprint assessments are conducted on the entire process to identify and implement ongoing sustainability improvements.
