The sales clerks in a bank can utilize their software frontend to receive credit-scoring for a customer, initiating a process in the banking system that communicates digitally with the agency in the background. This process sends an electronic scoring request to the agency immediately after initiation, minimizing paper use and reducing waste. The agency conducts a first quick scoring (level 1), which often results in an immediate digital response returned to the banking system within seconds. This rapid communication reduces energy consumption associated with traditional methods.

In instances where immediate scoring is not possible, the agency notifies the banking process of the delay via automated messaging and begins level 2 scoring, which may take several minutes. This digital notification eliminates the need for physical communication and further decreases carbon emissions. Once the scoring result is determined, it is transmitted back to the banking process electronically. The banking system then displays a message to the clerk, advising them to check back later if there is a delay.

To further enhance sustainability, the bank can implement renewable energy sources for its operations and ensure that all systems are optimized for energy efficiency. The entire process should be designed to minimize energy use and avoid production waste by employing green software practices, such as optimizing code for lower energy consumption during processing tasks. Additionally, any materials used in this process should be recyclable or sourced from sustainable suppliers to support circular economy principles. By digitizing all communications and processes, the bank not only reduces its carbon footprint but also aligns with modern sustainability goals.
