The sales clerks in a bank can utilize a digital software frontend to initiate the credit-scoring process for a customer. This triggers an automated process within the banking system that communicates electronically with the scoring agency, eliminating the need for physical document transfers. The process sends a digital scoring request to the agency immediately upon initiation. The agency conducts a preliminary quick scoring (level 1) using energy-efficient computing resources, which often results in an immediate response returned to the banking system within seconds.

In instances where immediate scoring is not feasible, the agency notifies the banking system of the delay through an electronic message and commences a more comprehensive level 2 scoring, which may require additional processing time but is conducted on renewable energy-powered servers to minimize carbon emissions. After determining the scoring result, the agency sends this information back to the banking system digitally.

The banking process then presents the result to the clerk at their frontend interface. If there is a delay, the system automatically updates the clerk with a digital notification advising them to check back later, thereby reducing unnecessary follow-up communications. Once the final result is received, it is displayed on the frontend without any paper waste or physical interaction.

This sustainable approach emphasizes **green software** principles by optimizing energy use through cloud-based services powered by renewable energy sources, minimizing waste through digital transactions, and enhancing efficiency via automated notifications. By adopting these practices, the bank significantly reduces its carbon footprint while maintaining effective customer service.
