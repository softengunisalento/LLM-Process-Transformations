Here's a more sustainable and digitalized version of the BPMN process description:

When goods need to be shipped, the secretary uses a digital logistics management system to determine the most eco-friendly shipping method. For large shipments, the system automatically sends requests for proposals to three pre-vetted sustainable logistics companies that use electric or low-emission vehicles. The system analyzes the offers based on carbon footprint, cost, and efficiency, then recommends the best option to the secretary for final approval. For small shipments, the system selects a carbon-neutral postal service. The secretary generates a digital shipping label through the system, which also calculates the need for insurance based on the shipment value and destination. If required, the logistics department head approves the insurance digitally. Meanwhile, warehouse staff use reusable or biodegradable packaging materials to prepare the goods, optimizing package size to minimize waste and transport emissions. The warehouse management system tracks packaging material usage and suggests improvements to reduce waste. Once everything is ready, the system notifies the chosen logistics company for pickup, providing them with all necessary digital documentation. Throughout the process, all communications and document transfers are handled electronically to minimize paper use. The entire workflow is powered by energy-efficient green software running on servers using renewable energy sources. The system continuously monitors and reports on the carbon footprint of each shipment, allowing for ongoing optimization of the shipping process to further reduce environmental impact.
