Here's a more sustainable and digitalized version of the BPMN process description:

When goods need to be shipped, the secretary uses a digital logistics management system to determine the most eco-friendly shipping method. For large shipments, the system automatically invites three sustainable logistics companies to submit digital offers, prioritizing those using electric or low-emission vehicles. The secretary selects the most efficient option based on carbon footprint calculations provided by the system. For smaller shipments, the system recommends local carbon-neutral delivery services or consolidated shipping to minimize trips. The secretary generates a digital shipping label through the system, which also assesses the need for parcel insurance based on predefined criteria, automatically notifying the logistics department head for approval if required. Simultaneously, warehouse staff receive digital instructions to package the goods using recyclable or biodegradable materials. They utilize energy-efficient packaging equipment powered by on-site renewable energy sources. The system tracks and optimizes packaging to minimize waste and maximize space efficiency. Once everything is prepared, the warehouse management system generates a pickup notification for the selected logistics company, providing them with all necessary digital documentation. Throughout the process, cloud-based software with green coding practices is used to minimize energy consumption of digital operations. The entire workflow is paperless, with all communications and approvals handled through energy-efficient mobile devices and low-power consumption displays. The system continuously monitors and reports on the carbon footprint of each shipment, allowing for ongoing optimization of the shipping process to further reduce environmental impact.
