Here's a more sustainable and digitalized version of the BPMN process description:

When goods need to be shipped, the secretary initiates a digital workflow in the company's green cloud-based logistics management system. The system automatically calculates the carbon footprint of different shipping options. For large shipments, the system sends out digital requests for proposals to three pre-vetted eco-friendly logistics companies that use electric or low-emission vehicles. These companies submit their offers through the platform, which uses AI to analyze and rank them based on sustainability metrics, cost-efficiency, and delivery time. The secretary reviews the AI-generated recommendations and selects the most sustainable option. For small shipments, the system recommends the most eco-friendly postal service available, prioritizing those using renewable energy in their operations. The secretary then generates a digital shipping label and, if necessary, the logistics department head authorizes a digital parcel insurance through the same platform. Meanwhile, warehouse staff receive digital instructions to package the goods using recycled and biodegradable materials. They use energy-efficient equipment powered by on-site solar panels to minimize the packaging process's carbon footprint. The system tracks and optimizes packaging to reduce waste and ensure efficient use of space. Once everything is prepared, the warehouse management module updates the status, and the selected logistics company is automatically notified for pickup. The entire process is paperless, utilizing energy-efficient servers and green software practices to minimize the digital carbon footprint. The system continuously monitors and reports on sustainability metrics, allowing for ongoing optimization of the shipping process to further reduce environmental impact.
